# Ablation of the corrected protocol — 2026-09-30

First measurements of the corrected MOESI-D branch. One simulator build serves
every column; columns differ only by environment knobs and by which guest
binary (fenced or unfenced AADD) is run. Every run passed its oracle **and** the
`run_matrix.py` coverage gate (required delegated / local / reject / retry events
nonzero for its mode). Raw stats, `config.ini`, guest output and manifests with
binary/patch hashes are under the ablation output directory on the run host;
the driver is `tools/ablate.sh`.

Setup: gem5 v25.1.0.1 + complete patch, `X86O3CPU`, 4 cores, 4 L2 banks,
128 B lines, 32 kB L1, 256 kB L2, Garnet crossbar, `se.py --ruby`. Knob sets:

| label | DSTATE_QUEUE_DEPTH | DSTATE_INIT_INTERVAL | DSTATE_BUSY_STALL | DSTATE_EXEC_LATENCY |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| default (as shipped) | 1 | = latency | 0 | 42 |
| queue8 | 8 | = latency | 0 | 42 |
| pipe4 | 8 | 4 | 0 | 42 |
| full | 8 | 4 | 1 | 42 |
| full_l20 | 8 | 4 | 1 | 20 |

Modes: `local` = same binary, `DSTATE_ENABLED=0` (matched-ISA conventional
baseline); `remote` = home execution, no persistence; `persistent` = home
execution with the D retention policy. `forced-nack` passed everywhere as a
correctness control and is omitted below.

**Read every time with the caveat that it is whole-program `simSeconds` with no
ROI**: OpenMP start-up and the serial oracle are included. Relative comparisons
between modes of one binary are sound; on the 8,000-add `scatter` runs the fixed
cost dominates and differences of ~1% are noise.

Reading: as shipped, 57% of updates are rejected because one accepted operation
per *bank* is not enough for four cores over four banks, and each rejection costs
a wasted round trip plus a migration — slower than not delegating at all. With
bounded admission and pipelining, every update is delegated, no update is
rejected, and network flits fall 7.6×. Runtime then depends only on per-update
service latency: 21% slower than the baseline at 42 cycles, 17% faster at 20.
Persistence (the D policy) changes nothing here because no reads occur.

Reading: **removing the fences is worth ~16% on every path, including the
conventional one** (0.000250 → 0.000210). Queue depth and pipelining alone
change nothing because nothing is accepted; busy-stall takes delegation from
1 of 8,000 to 8,000 of 8,000 with 12.6% fewer flits — but on a single hot line
a core that owns the line batches many adds for free, while delegation pays the
bank's service latency per add, serialized. Ownership is implicit combining.
Break-even is near 20 cycles; beating ownership on one line needs combining
(one read-modify-write for N queued adds) or a hot-word register.

## Round 2 (second build, 16:19): store-class micro-op, hot-word buffer, combining, relaxed ordering, delegate-from-S

Same day, same worktree, five more mechanisms. Four are knobs that default off;
the fifth — the AADD micro-op is now *store-class* (`IsStore`), so the ROB retires
it at commit and the store-queue entry, not the core, waits for the remote ACK — is
on in every run of this build. Its effect is therefore read from the `local` and
`default` rows against the round-1 tables above. Knob sets, each layered on `full`
(queue 8, interval 4, busy-stall):

| label | adds | models |
|---|---|---|
| full_l20 | `DSTATE_EXEC_LATENCY=20` | round-1 best |
| hot | `DSTATE_HOTWORDS=8 DSTATE_HIT_LATENCY=4` | recently updated words register-resident at the bank; misses pay 42 |
| hotmerge | hot + `DSTATE_MERGE_LIMIT=8` | queued same-word adds fold into the executing one; each still gets its own ACK |
| hotmerge_relaxed | hotmerge + `DSTATE_RELAXED_AMO=1` | no-return atomics leave TSO's one-store-in-flight rule (Intel RAO-INT ordering) |
| hotmerge_fromS | hotmerge + `DSTATE_DELEGATE_FROM_S=1` | an L1 holding the line read-only delegates and drops its copy |
| hot32_merge[_relaxed] | as hotmerge[_relaxed] with 32 entries | buffer covers the 32-counter working set |

`DSTATE_RELAXED_AMO` changes the **CPU**, not the protocol, and `run_matrix.py`
applies it to the `local` mode too. A relaxed row is compared with the relaxed
`local` row; comparing it with the TSO `local` row would credit the protocol with
a gain that belongs to the ordering contract.

Reading, in order of the rows:

1. **Store-class retirement is worth 8% to the conventional path and 12% to the
   delegated one** (0.024766 → 0.021692); the core no longer idles at the ROB head
   while the add is outstanding. This recovers the benefit v1.0.0's early
   completion was after, in a form that keeps the ordering guarantee: the store
   buffer, not the protocol, holds the completion.
2. **An 8-entry hot-word buffer buys nothing on 512 lines** (128 lines per bank
   against 8 entries) and the 4-cycle hits pull the lock-step cores into a convoy
   on the same line: 3.07M busy-stalls, time equal to the 42-cycle case.
3. **Combining halves the bank's work and changes the time by nothing.** 2.05M of
   4.1M adds were folded into another operation and `Busy` fell to 0, yet
   `simSeconds` is identical. Under TSO each core has at most one add in flight,
   so the bank never has more than four requests: runtime is per-core round-trip
   latency, not bank throughput, and merging cannot shorten a round trip.
4. **Relaxed ordering is where combining pays.** With many adds in flight per
   core (the O3 store queue; `blockedByCache` rose from 6 to 1.0M on the local
   path, i.e. the L1's outstanding-request limit now binds), the conventional path
   gets 3.7× from overlapping its migrations, and the delegated path gets 11.8× —
   **3.18× over the matched relaxed conventional path with 5.4× fewer flits**.
   2.7% of adds were rejected when the depth-8 bank queue was full and fell back
   to GETX; the forced-NACK control (every add rejected) still finishes within 11%
   of conventional.
5. Persistence again changed nothing (no reads in this workload).

## Round 3 (2026-10-01 build): requester-side combining, bank back-pressure, core and working-set scaling, phased read/update

Two mechanisms, both default-off, on top of the round-2 knobs
(`tools/ablate.sh v3`; the matching validation record is in
[`docs/VALIDATION.md`](../docs/VALIDATION.md)):

- **Requester-side combining** (`DSTATE_REQ_COMBINE=16`). gem5's Sequencer queues
  every request to a line behind the one already outstanding, so a core never had
  more than one delegated add per line in flight whatever the ordering mode
  allowed — the reason relaxed ordering did nothing for a single hot word in
  round 2. When the outstanding request completes, the Sequencer now issues the
  consecutive run of queued same-word adds as one request with their summed
  operand; all complete on its ACK. It is the delegated-path counterpart of the
  batching an owning core gets for free.
- **Back-pressure** (`DSTATE_QUEUE_STALL=1`). A request that finds the bank executor
  full waits in the input buffer for a released slot instead of being NACKed into
  a full ownership migration.

Columns: **T** = TSO, 20-cycle service, no hot-word buffer (round-2 best TSO +
both mechanisms, queue 16, merge limit 64); **TH** = T with 42/4-cycle service and
32 hot words (single-line TSO comparison); **R** = relaxed ordering, 42/4-cycle
service, 32 hot words + both mechanisms. A relaxed row is compared with the
relaxed `local` row. Rows are `local,remote,persistent` unless a forced-NACK
control is shown.

## Round 4 (2026-10-01 build): far reads, delta-line requests, static-placement oracle, 16-core sweeps, the paper's applications

Two mechanisms aimed at the regimes that were still losing, both default-off:

- **Far reads** (`DSTATE_FAR_READS=1`). A load that reaches a line held in D is
  answered with a snapshot and no sharer is recorded, so the line never leaves
  the home and the next update needs no invalidation. A line read
  `DSTATE_READ_DOWNGRADE` times with no update in between falls back to ordinary
  cached copies (64 in these runs). This is the first mechanism that gives the D
  state a job only it can do: far reads exist only for retained lines.
- **Delta-line requests** (`DSTATE_DELTA_MIN_WORDS=2`). When a core's queued adds
  span several words of one line, the Sequencer sends one masked request with
  every word's summed delta (32–144 B by word count); the home applies all words
  in one operation. Bank-side merging now uses the same masks.

Columns: **T4 / TH4 / R4** = the round-3 T / TH / R knob sets plus far reads
(plus delta lines in R4). Gates on the build: fenced regression under R4 4/4,
unfenced regression under T4 (O3) and TH4 (Minor) 4/4, unfenced regression under
R4 4/4 (contract check; it passed this time), litmus L20–L24 0 forbidden; the
regression exercised 2,062–2,093 far reads per run.

(379k far reads at threshold 1 against 46k at threshold 4) but does not help
time: each read now costs a round trip to the home instead of an L1 hit, and
under TSO it also waits for the core's own preceding add to be acknowledged, so
the add latency that fire-and-forget hides is exposed on every read. Far reads
pay only when many adds are in flight — the relaxed column — where the read's
wait overlaps them. Delegate-from-S does not change that picture. The mesh rows answer
the topology question: on a 4×4 mesh the conventional path at 16 cores slows by
2.1× against the crossbar (every migration now crosses hops) while the delegated
path slows by 4%, so the many-line advantage grows from 2.5× to **4.95×**. The
two-hop home path is what makes the mechanism topology-insensitive.

## Round 6 (2026-10-02): admission gate, same-line batching under TSO, ACK-with-value, promotion on writers

Three mechanisms aimed at the strict-ordering losses, each a knob defaulting off:

- **Admission gate** (`DSTATE_MIN_WRITERS`, `DSTATE_WRITER_EPOCH`): the home keeps a
  bounded per-bank table of which cores have written each line in the current
  epoch of updates and accepts a delegated add only once that many *distinct*
  cores have; otherwise it rejects, and the requester takes the line by the
  ordinary GETX path and owns it. The epoch (updates, not cycles) is the exit: a
  line that goes quiet or single-writer is rejected again and ownership resumes.
  The table lives in the engine, not the cache entry — a line the home does not
  hold has no entry, yet its rejected writers must be counted (the first version
  kept the bitmap in the entry and admitted nothing; found by the coverage gate).
- **Same-line batching under TSO** (`DSTATE_TSO_SAMELINE`, O3): a younger no-return
  add to the *same line* may leave the store queue while older ones to that line
  are in flight; stores to any other line still wait for all of them. The
  Sequencer sends the queued adds as one request and the home applies them as one
  operation, so no observer can see them out of program order (`ordering_litmus2`
  checks exactly this).
- **ACK carries the value** (`DSTATE_ACK_VALUE`): the terminal ACK carries the
  updated word (16 B class, still one flit); a load of that word waiting behind
  the add completes from it instead of making its own trip.
- **Promotion on writers** (`DSTATE_PROMOTE_WRITERS`): promote to D once that many
  distinct writers are seen, so far reads can engage under strict ordering.

mechanisms estimate this **owner hit rate** (both knobs, default off; see
`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §4a):

- **Change-rate gate** at the home (`DSTATE_MIN_CHANGE_PCT`, `DSTATE_MIN_CHANGES`):
  the bank counts each line's would-be ownership changes per update, a GETX
  from a non-owner while conventional and an update from a core other than the
  previous writer while delegated, so the estimate stays alive while the line is
  delegated. Counters halve every epoch of updates (`DSTATE_WRITER_EPOCH`) and
  restart after `DSTATE_WRITER_IDLE` quiet cycles; `DSTATE_ADAPTIVE_GATE` steps the
  threshold per bank with a cooling-off period.
- **Owner-tenure predictor** at the requester (`DSTATE_MAX_TENURE`,
  `DSTATE_TENURE_PROBE`, `DSTATE_TENURE_IDLE`): the L1 records how many adds it
  performed on a line it owned before another core took it, and delegates only
  lines whose last tenure was at most the threshold. A line with no record is
  owned first (probed) so the tenure can be measured, and records expire so
  delegated lines are re-probed.

functional-read priorities so gem5 reads the L2 copy or memory instead, as the

only fix by rejecting everything): the change-rate gate alone takes it from +24 %
to +12 %, and with time-based decay (`DSTATE_WRITER_IDLE`, lines that go quiet
restart as single-writer) to **+5.0 %**; adaptation adds nothing here (+5.5 %).
The tenure predictor with a 1M-cycle re-probe period leaves it at +11 % because
the sketch's hot set shifts every 128 inserts and a line that was contended when
probed stays delegated until its record expires; re-probing every 100k cycles
brings it to +1.4 %, and **predictor plus gate together make it a tie (+0.3 %)**:
the gate refuses the lines the home can see are single-writer, the predictor
the lines whose owner would have kept hitting them.

**512 scattered lines** keep their win under every home-side setting (−21 % with
the gate, −21 % with adaptation) and lose a little to probing (−19 % at a
1M-cycle re-probe period, −10 % at 100k: each probe is an ownership and a few
migrations per line). The cost of the predictor is therefore set by how often
it re-probes, and the setting that fixes the sketch (100k) halves the win here;
the combined policy at 1M keeps −19 %.

Where the predictor **hurts**: the single hot word and the 32-counter line were
ties with batching, and the predictor turns them into +6 % and +12 % losses.
Their owner tenure at 4 cores is about four adds, so the predictor correctly
reads "owner wins", but the few delegations it still issues (3,336 of 800,000)
recall the line from its owner, and the probes themselves cost a migration
each. The gate leaves both at a tie. The read-mixed line improves from +40 % to
+24 % (remote) with the predictor and does not move with the gate; it stays a
loss at 4 cores.

At 16 cores the two mechanisms part ways. The **change-rate gate costs nothing**:
512 lines 2.26× with zero rejections (the writer gate's epoch had cut this to
1.79×), phased 2.29× (was 1.72× under the writer gate), the hot word under
strict ordering 1.62× (1.58× in round 6), read-mixed 1.71×, the sketch +2 % as
without any gate. Every line at 16 cores changes hands on nearly every update,
the ratio the home measures says so, and the gate admits them.

The **tenure predictor does not scale**: it keeps the 512-line win (2.23×) but
turns the hot word from a 1.6× win into an **18 % loss** and the read-mixed line
from 1.71× into a tie, delegating only 868 and 761 of 800,000 updates. With
sixteen requesters queued at the home, an owner gets two or three adds in
before the recall arrives, so its measured tenure exceeds the threshold of one
and the predictor reads "owner wins" where the owner loses. A threshold that
admits these lines would admit the sketch at 4 cores. The requester's view is
local by construction; the home's count of ownership changes is the signal that
is right at both scales.

What this says about the design: use the **change-rate gate with time decay**
as the admission policy (it never costs a measured win, and takes the sketch
from +24 % to +5 %); treat the tenure predictor as a 4-core refinement that
closes the last 5 % on the sketch at the price of the hot word, and never

### Speculation oracle

With `DSTATE_DELEGATE_FROM_S` the L1 keeps a shared copy while its add is
delegated; the Sequencer predicts the word the add will produce (copy plus every
add of its own folded into the request) and checks it against the ACK value. A
right prediction could have served a load behind the add without waiting; a
wrong one would have been a pipeline squash.

### Reading round 8

**Cheap rejection** earns its place, but only in its third form. Served for
every refusal, it made CMS at 4 cores a near tie (+0.8 %) and left the many-line
and hot-word rows alone, yet took the 16-core read-mixed run from 1.71× to
+44 %: the far reads that carried that win vanished (297,000 → 0) because every
transient refusal at a busy home became a migration. Excluding retained lines
changed nothing at 16 cores; the lines were taken before they were ever
retained. Serving only the **gate's own decision** as a GETX, with structural
refusals NACKing as before, keeps everything: at 4 cores CMS is a tie
(**+0.03 %** retained, +0.8 % remote), the hot word and the 32-counter line stay
ties, 512 lines stays −21 %, and read-mixed improves from +40 % to **+35 %**; at
16 cores 512 lines 2.26×, hot word 1.62×, read-mixed **1.71×**, CMS a tie, all
unchanged from the best round-7 settings, now with the stricter 90 % gate.

**The stricter gate is free once rejection is cheap.** At 50 % the gate admitted
the Count-Min sketch; at 90 % it refuses it, and because a refusal now costs
one trip the refusal is no more expensive than the owner's own miss. Every
16-core row clears 90 % as easily as 50 % (every line there changes hands on
nearly every update).

**Promotion had to come back.** Removing promotion on writers as "marginal" was
measured only on 4-core rows; the 16-core read-mixed win depended on it. Its
replacement promotes on the gate table's own evidence and reproduces the
round-7 row exactly (0.002350 ms).

**The speculation oracle says no.** With the L1 holding a shared copy, a load
behind a delegated add could have been served from copy + delta 43 % of the time
on read-mixed at 4 cores, 6 % at 16 cores, and never on the hot word (0 of
539,510): on the lines contended enough to delegate, another core's update
reaches the home first almost every time. Speculative forwarding would squash
more often than it forwards; it is not built.

**Where the design stands under strict ordering (x86 today), 4 cores:** 512
lines −21 %, Count-Min sketch tie, hot word tie, 32 counters tie, read-mixed
+35 % (the one open loss: the load behind the add waits for the home's round
trip, and no admission policy changes that). **16 cores:** 512 lines 2.26×, hot
word 1.62×, read-mixed 1.71×, phased 2.27–2.29×, sketch tie. Relaxed ordering
keeps its 3.3–4.9× on the many-line cases. Hardware: a 256-entry gate table per
L2 bank (about 6 KB); nothing in the L1 beyond the protocol states; no new
messages or message fields since round 6's 16-byte ACK.

### Reading round 9

**The open loss is the round trip, not the executor.** Read-mixed at 4 cores
moves from +30 % to +40 % as the executor's service time goes from 10 to 30
cycles, so the 20-cycle assumption is worth about five points either way; what
the load behind the add waits for is the network. The hot-word buffer does not
help this workload and slightly hurts it (+46–49 % with it, +35 % without),
which is why the strict set uses the buffer only for single-word workloads.
The many-line case is the opposite: it is executor-bound, and a 10-cycle
executor would take 512 lines at 4 cores from −21 % to −39 % and read-mixed at
16 cores from 1.72× to 2.03×. A circuit argument for a faster executor is the
one hardware lever left that moves the numbers.

**8 cores is the crossover.** Read-mixed goes from a 35 % loss at 4 cores to a
1.11× win at 8 (retained); 512 lines, the hot word and the sketch at 8 sit
between their 4- and 16-core neighbours.

**The recommended set holds everywhere it was measured.** At 4 cores the only
loss is read-mixed; the sketch (+0.9 %), the hot word, the 32-counter line, the
Zipfian counters and the graph kernel are ties, 512 lines −21 %, phased 1.26×
strict and 3.0× relaxed. At 16 cores every contended workload wins (512 lines
1.26× strict, 2.26× relaxed, 4.95× on the mesh; hot word 1.62×, 1.69× on the
mesh; read-mixed 1.72×; phased 2.29×) and the uncontended ones (sketch, Zipfian,
graph) are ties within 0.5 %. The admission gate at 90 % with cheap rejection
costs nothing on any winning row and is what makes the sketch and the Zipfian
counters ties rather than losses.

**ROI-only timing** changes two numbers in opposite directions. The 16-core hot
word is 2.94× inside the loop versus 1.62× whole-program (thread start and exit
dominate a 0.6 ms run), so the honest quote is "1.6–2.9×". The 16-core 512-line
relaxed case is 1.88× inside the loop versus 2.26× whole-program: part of the
whole-program win is the conventional run's slower start (its threads begin by
migrating 512 lines sixteen ways). Read-mixed is 1.75× either way. Whole-program
numbers are quoted throughout for consistency with rounds 1–8; where the two
differ by more than 10 % the text says so.

**What is not here.** No ROI-instrumented sketch (the libm5 build lacks it; its
whole-program number is a tie). No 32- or 64-core rows for the final set; the
round-4 and round-5 mesh rows (4.87× at 32, 3.23× at 64 cores, relaxed) stand
on the pre-gate protocol, whose 16-core relaxed result the final set reproduces
exactly, so they are expected to hold but were not rerun.

## What the numbers support

1. The AADD fence removal is a uniform ~16% gain and is safe under TSO on this
   evidence (litmus + regression).
2. As shipped, the corrected protocol was slower than the matched conventional
   path in every workload, because its one-op-per-bank admission rejected most
   updates into fallback migrations.
3. Bounded admission + pipelining + transient-state waiting make delegation
   complete (100% on `scatter_multi`, 0 rejections) and cut network flits 7.6×.
4. With admission fixed, per-update service latency is the only remaining wall:
   ±38% runtime between 42 and 20 cycles on `scatter_multi`; a 17% win at 20.
5. On a single hot line, ownership-based batching beats per-update delegation
   unless service is ≲20 cycles. Combining at the bank is the structural answer.
6. Persistence (D) showed no incremental benefit in any configuration measured
   here, including read-interleaving at 20-cycle service, where it delegated more
   yet ran 6% slower with more flits than home execution without retention. On
   this evidence the defensible mechanism is bounded-admission home execution of
   no-return atomics; the persistent state is a cost.
7. The 512-line result reproduces at 2,048 lines (4× the working set): shipped
   +28% and 26% rejected; corrected −17%, 100% delegated, 0.14× flits.

Round 2 adds:

8. Making the AADD a store-class micro-op recovers the core-side half of
   fire-and-forget legally: −8% conventional, −12% delegated, oracle and litmus
   intact, because the ROB retires at commit and the store queue holds the ACK.
9. Under TSO the bank is never the bottleneck (≤1 add in flight per core), so
   neither the hot-word buffer nor combining moves the 512-line time; the TSO
   result stands at −21% with 7.6× fewer flits.
10. Under the relaxed no-return ordering Intel RAO-INT specifies, applied to both
    sides, the 512-line workload runs **3.18× faster than the matched conventional
    path with 5.4× fewer flits** (0.002321 vs 0.007378 s), every cell correct,
    2.7% of adds rejected into fallback. This is the headline, and it is
    conditional on that ordering contract and on combining at the bank.
11. On a single hot word or line the conventional owner wins in every
    configuration: +38–43% with hot-word + combining, +324% without, and worse
    (+60% to +224%) once reads are interleaved, where only 5–30% of adds are
    delegated because reads pull the line back into the L1.
12. Persistence (the D policy) still shows no benefit anywhere and a cost under
    reads. The defensible mechanism remains bounded-admission home execution of
    no-return atomics, now with combining and store-class retirement.
13. The gains scale with the number of distinct hot lines, not with contention
    on one line — the opposite of the historical paper's framing.

Round 3 adds:

14. Back-pressure (or an executor queue of 16) removes every executor-full
    rejection: 512 lines under relaxed ordering go from 3.18× to **3.35×** over the
    matched conventional path with 6.8× fewer flits, and 2.96–3.29× from 2,048 to
    8,192 lines. Under TSO the many-line result is −20% to −21% at every working
    set and every core count measured (4, 8, 16).
15. Requester-side combining lifts the single-hot-word ceiling the Sequencer had
    imposed: from +37% slower to a **tie at 4 cores**, −1% at 8 and **1.62× faster
    at 16 cores** under relaxed ordering. Under TSO a core never queues two adds,
    so the hot word stays slower there (+43% at 4 cores, +13% at 8).
16. Combining is same-word: a 32-counter rotation and the read-interleaved single
    line are unchanged and remain the owner's regimes at 4–8 cores.
17. The retention policy (D) shows its first measured benefit on the phased
    update/read workload it was designed for: 1% (4 cores, TSO), 6.5% (4 cores,
    relaxed) and **33% at 16 cores** over home execution without retention, with
    3–4% fewer flits. It still adds nothing on update-only workloads and still
    costs under fine-grained read interleaving on one line.
18. At 16 cores the relaxed many-line gain becomes bank-bound (2.26× with four
    banks, 87% of adds merged); bank count, not core count, is the next lever.
19. The conventional baseline itself needs 256-entry buffers to run 16 cores on
    4 banks under TSO; the 16-core rows use them in every mode.

Round 4 adds:

20. **Far reads give the D state its job.** The read-interleaved single line, the
    worst regime through round 3, goes from +224% to +18% at 4 cores and to
    **2.20× faster** than the owner at 16 cores under relaxed ordering; without
    retention (no D, so no far reads) the same runs stay 30–94% slower. Phased
    workloads gain another 1–3 points for D.
21. **Delta-line requests close the multi-word case**: the 32-counter rotation goes
    from +73% to a tie, with 800,000 adds carried in 355,000 requests.
22. **The hot word crosses over under strict ordering too**: 1.38× at 16 cores
    (TSO), after +43% at 4 and +13% at 8. Under relaxed ordering it stays 1.62×.
23. The 16-core many-line ceiling is not the per-core request limit (16, 32 and 64
    are identical); 8 banks instead of 4 lift it from 2.26× to 2.49×.
24. gem5's Garnet lacked the unmasked functional read that a syscall's memory
    access needs when the only copy of a line is in flight; the patch adds it.
    It was hit by the conventional path under relaxed ordering (CMS), not by
    the delegated one.
25. Far reads are a relaxed-ordering mechanism in practice: under TSO they can be
    made to engage (promotion threshold 1) but each read then exposes the
    preceding add's full latency, and the read-interleaved line stays slower than
    the owner at 4 cores. Promotion-threshold and delegate-from-S variants do not
    change that.
26. Topology: on a 4×4 mesh at 16 cores (8 banks, 8 directories) the 512-line
    workload runs **4.95×** faster than conventional under relaxed ordering
    (2.48× on the crossbar with the same counts): ownership migration pays for
    every hop, home execution does not.
